# Chapter 28 — Past Verbs in Their Sentence Patterns

**Status:** Proposed; not implemented or approved

**Scope:** Chapter 28 map and five current lesson fixtures, one new cumulative review, and one distinct chapter test. Preserve the current five lesson IDs; repurpose `ch28-l05` as the fifth teaching lesson and add `ch28-l06` and `ch28-test`.

## 1. Decision and learning outcome

Keep Chapter 28 after the preposition unit and before Chapter 29's nominal-versus-verbal sentence comparison. Reframe it from a second introduction to past tense into **new common verbs and the sentence patterns they use**. Learners have already encountered past actions, doers, objects, and a basic feminine past form in Chapter 17. Chapter 28 should retrieve those skills briefly, then teach the new verbs through their meanings and complements.

By the end, learners should be able to:

1. Understand eight new verbs in familiar past-tense sentence frames: **عَلِمَ، فَهِمَ، حَفِظَ، رَضِيَ، أَتَى، أَعْطَى، جَمَعَ، بَدَأَ**.
2. Identify the doer and the verb's complement in a complete sentence.
3. Distinguish a direct object from a phrase governed by a preposition, including **رَضِيَ عَنْ / بِـ** and **أَتَى إِلَى** in taught examples.
4. Read **أَعْطَى** with the recipient and the thing given; recognize a Quranic first-person form such as **أَعْطَيْنَاكَ** without treating it as identical in person to **أَعْطَى**.
5. Apply the familiar third-person feminine past pattern to selected verbs, with the spelling changes in weak-final verbs shown explicitly and no claim that one suffix rule describes every Arabic past-tense form.
6. Read one or more verified Quran excerpts for the exact verb or verb pattern under study.
7. Build a short action sequence that prepares for Chapter 29's sentence-type comparison without teaching that chapter's main distinction early.

It is relevant here because this chapter can analyze its actual giving verb and attached pronouns. ## 5. Assessment blueprint

Use a 12-question test with the configured chapter-test pass threshold (80%, or 10/12, if that remains the project standard when implementation begins).

| Outcome | Questions |
|---|---:|
| Understand a new verb in context | 2 |
| Identify doer and direct object | 2 |

Do not test root trivia for roots not taught, verse-number recall, interpretive claims, or the full conjugation of weak verbs. Assessment choices must be fair in both English and Urdu; no item should award a correct answer solely because it appears first or because all true/false prompts share one key.

**Corrections**

1. **Al-Kawthar 108:1 is Chapter 24's anchor**, and Chapter 24 already taught **ـنَا** on **إِنَّ** versus **ـنَا** on a verb. Lesson 3 retrieves that ("you met **أَعْطَيْنَاكَ** in Chapter 24") and adds only the new part: giver, recipient and gift with **أَعْطَى**.
2. The feminine past **ـتْ** was first taught in **Chapter 8**, then read in Chapter 17; retrieve from both.
3. **أَتَى** takes its destination most often as a direct object (**أَتَى الْمَسْجِدَ**); **أَتَى إِلَى** also occurs in modern Arabic. Teach the direct-object frame first and accept **إِلَى** as a variant — never score one against the other.
4. Verified anchors: 9:100 **رَضِيَ اللَّهُ عَنْهُمْ**, 32:7 **وَبَدَأَ خَلْقَ الْإِنسَانِ مِنْ طِينٍ**, 104:2 **الَّذِي جَمَعَ مَالًا وَعَدَّدَهُ**. The 2:151 hook's **تَعْلَمُونَ** is a glossed chunk only (S11).
