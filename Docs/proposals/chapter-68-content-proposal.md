# Chapter 68 — Jussive Particles and the Present Verb

**Status:** Proposal only — not approved or implemented

## Recommendation

Retain Chapter 68 as the focused unit on particles that make a single imperfect verb **مجزوم**, but rewrite it substantially. The fixtures currently teach several core errors, including that bare imperative verbs are governed by an invisible **لام الأمر**, that an imperfect verb after **مَا** in a relative clause is jussive, and that an ordinary final fatḥa changes into kasra under jazm. The repeated “complete system” claim is also too broad: this unit covers four common particles governing one imperfect verb, not every conditional construction involving jussive verbs.

Use the sequence to teach the effect of jazm, the four particles and their meanings, the three common jussive signs, the temporal-versus-jussive **لَمَّا** distinction, and the difference between **لام الأمر** and **فعل الأمر**. Then hand off to Chapter 69 for the jussive response to a request/condition, without promising that Chapter 68 alone makes every Qur’anic jussive form understandable.

## Proposed learning outcomes

By the end of the chapter, learners should be able to:

- Identify the four common particles that put a following imperfect verb in the jussive when used to govern one verb: **لَمْ، لَمَّا، لَا النَّاهِيَة، لَام الأمر**.
- Describe the main meanings of each without conflating prohibitive **لَا** with ordinary negation or temporal **لَمَّا** with “not yet.”
- Recognize the common jussive signs: sukūn for a sound-final imperfect, deletion of the weak final letter, and deletion of **ن** in the five verbs.
- Distinguish an imperfect verb preceded by **لَام الأمر** from the separate, built direct imperative (**فعل الأمر**).
- Parse a short, exact Qur’anic example and transfer the rule to an authored sentence.
- Explain that other conditional constructions and more complex irregular forms require further study.

## Proposed lesson sequence

Use five instructional lessons, one formative review, and a separate final assessment. The added distinctions are necessary because the present lessons teach the system inaccurately; they are not extra advanced material for its own sake.

| Order | Lesson | Scope and practice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What jazm does: the three common signs | Introduce the imperfect verb’s jussive state and distinguish it from indicative and subjunctive forms already encountered. Teach sound-final **يَذْهَبُ → لَمْ يَذْهَبْ**, one reviewed weak-final example, and one five-verb example such as a form ending in **ـونَ → ـوا** after a jussive trigger. Clearly label the changed ending and avoid calling it noun case. |
| 2 | **لَمْ** and **لَمَّا**: “did not” vs “not yet” | Use simple authored pairs and the exact **لَمَّا يَأْتِكُمْ** excerpt from 2:214. Explain that the perfect-form temporal **لَمَّا** meaning “when” is a different use, previewed with the exact **وَلَمَّا وَرَدَ مَاءَ مَدْيَنَ** from 28:23. Learners identify what follows each form and whether jazm occurs. |
| 3 | **لَا النَّاهِيَة** vs ordinary negative **لَا** | Contrast **لَا تَفْعَلْ** (“do not do”) with **لَا يَفْعَلُ** (“he does not do”). Use **لَا تَيْأَسُوا مِنْ رَوْحِ اللَّهِ** (12:87) as a fully referenced example and an authored neutral example for productive practice. Include correct plural **حذف النون** analysis. |
| 4 | **لَام الأمر** vs direct imperative | Explicitly contrast **لِيَكْتُبْ** (“let him write,” lām + imperfect jussive) with **اُكْتُبْ** (“write!”, direct imperative form). State that the direct command is not made from an invisible lām and is not itself an imperfect in the jussive. Use Quranic **لِيُنْفِقْ** (65:7) only after exact text and parse are checked. |
| 5 | Read and use the jussive in context | Work through a small, accurately sourced set—one example for each of the four triggers—then a short authored dialogue that asks for or gives a request. Point forward to Chapter 69’s **جواب الطلب**. Include one contrast with a conditional construction only if its trigger and verb analysis are stated precisely. |
| 6 | Formative review: trigger, meaning, form | Mixed retrieval: name the trigger, classify the verb as imperfect or imperative, mark the ending, distinguish temporal **لَمَّا**, and repair a misanalysis. Give explanatory feedback. Do not repeat the review verbatim in the assessment. |
| 7 | Chapter 68 final assessment | Add a distinct `REVIEW` lesson with the canonical `assessment` payload. Test particle recognition, all three jussive signs, **لَا** and **لَمَّا** contrasts, and the imperative distinction. Use at least one unfamiliar but vetted sentence for transfer. |

## Curriculum continuity

- **Chapter 67 → 68:** Explicitly recall that **لَوْ** is non-jussive, then introduce the particles in this chapter as a focused set that do cause jazm. If **إِنْ** is introduced as a conditional particle, mark it as a separate two-verb conditional construction and teach its mechanics only in the designated later unit.
- **Chapter 68 → 69:** Close by previewing that the response to a command/request can be jussive. Chapter 69 must distinguish the response construction from the four particles governing a single verb and verify that each chosen verse/example actually has a jussive response. Avoid claiming Chapter 68 has completed every use of jazm.
- **Earlier present-tense work:** Retrieve the indicative and subjunctive endings learned earlier, but do not repeat their full lessons. Use them as contrasts in the first lesson.
- **Conversation practice:** Include one brief request-response exchange as application; keep the learning objective grammatical, not a duplicate general conversation chapter.

## Acceptance checks

1. No lesson says that **اتَّقِ، صِلْ، افْعَلْ، ذَرُوا** are imperfect verbs made jussive by an invisible **لام الأمر**.
2. The chapter distinguishes explicit **لَام الأمر + مضارع مجزوم** from direct **فعل الأمر**.
3. Jussive signs are accurately demonstrated for sound-final verbs, weak-final verbs, and the five verbs; no “fatḥa becomes kasra” claim remains.
4. **لَا الناهية**, ordinary negative **لَا**, jussive **لَمَّا**, and temporal **لَمَّا** have correct meanings and examples.
5. All Qur’anic quotations and hooks match their cited references, and every free-text Quranic quote is independently checked.
6. The “complete system” language is scoped to the particles and forms actually taught; conditional particles governing two verbs are clearly deferred or handled in their designated chapter.
7. The formative review and a distinct final `REVIEW` + `assessment` lesson are both present.
8. Fixture validation, Qur’anic text audit, and Urdu audit pass after implementation; a qualified Arabic review is documented.
