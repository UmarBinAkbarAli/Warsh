# Chapter 21 — Places and Movement: content repair proposal

**Status:** Approved with the review amendments in section 7 and implemented 2026-09-24  
**Scope:** Chapter 21 map, five existing fixtures/seed rows, one proposed Conversation Lab, and one proposed final test  
**Recommended structure:** Four teaching lessons, one Conversation Lab, one review, one distinct final test (seven items)

## 1. Decision and learner outcome

Keep the theme **Places and Movement**, but make this an **applied journey and landmark chapter**, not a second list of place nouns, prepositions, and position words. By the end, a learner should be able to:

1. ask or answer **مِنْ أَيْنَ؟** (“from where?”) and **إِلَى أَيْنَ؟** (“to where?”) in a supported third-person journey, locating its starting point and destination in **مِنْ ... إِلَى ...**;
2. distinguish **ذَهَبَ** (went), **خَرَجَ** (went out/left), **دَخَلَ** (entered), and **رَجَعَ** (returned) from context, without deriving a verb paradigm;
3. understand a destination relative to one or two familiar landmarks, including **بَيْنَ ... وَ ...** (“between ... and ...”), and answer **أَيْنَ؟** with more information than a one-word location;
4. recognize a genuinely occurring movement word in a short Quran excerpt, with enough supplied context to avoid inventing a destination or referent;
5. complete a coherent exchange about finding a person: hear where they left and went, then ask where that destination is; and
6. pass a separate server-graded chapter final test.

Do **not** teach full street navigation, imperative formation, new person/tense conjugations, or a detailed case system. Recognition of an imperative in a Quran quotation may be glossed, but it is not a production target.

## 3. Findings requiring repair

1. **The map's hook and parse are off-target.** The map opens with [An-Nahl 16:19](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=16&verse=19), **وَاللَّهُ يَعْلَمُ مَا تُسِرُّونَ وَمَا تُعْلِنُونَ**. It does not illustrate a place, route, or motion. Its parse labels **الْمَدِينَةِ** after **إِلَى** as **مضاف إليه**; here it is a noun governed by the preposition, not the second term of an idafa. Rewrite the hook, parse, focus records, and Noor tip together.
2. **Lesson 1 repeats place words and teaches a false gender rule.** The current grammar note says most place nouns are feminine and that **مَسْجِد** is “commonly referred to with feminine grammar.” **مَسْجِد** is masculine. The claim that definite **ال** itself causes a final kasra is also false; a noun's case is determined by its syntactic role. Replace the entire note, not just its example. Its [Yusuf 12:93](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=12&verse=93) hook does contain **اذْهَبُوا** (“go”), but not the listed city/village/market terms; use that verse only for a correctly framed movement-recognition task if retained.
3. **Lesson 2 repeats preposition glosses and invents a pronunciation rule.** **فِي** has no hidden shadda on **ي**. **مِنْ + الْـ** is not a simple word merger into **مِنَ الْـ**; connected pronunciation involves the following hamzat al-waṣl and vowel adjustment, which need not be taught here. A genitive noun does not *always visibly end in kasra*; keep beginner examples narrow and avoid universal claims. In [Al-Baqarah 2:125](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=2&verse=125), **مِن مَقَامِ إِبْرَاهِيمَ** occurs, but **إِلَى إِبْرَاهِيمَ** belongs to a covenant/charge, not a physical journey. The lesson names **الْكَعْبَة** although that word is not in the displayed ayah, and reveal indices `[1,3]` do not highlight **مِنْ** or **إِلَى**. Do not use this ayah as a from-place/to-place example.
4. **Lesson 3 turns useful patterns into absolutes.** **ذَهَبَ** does not *always need* **إِلَى**; **إِلَى** specifies a destination when one is supplied. **رَجَعَ** does not mean “first time versus one-way” compared with **ذَهَبَ**. **دَخَلَ الْمَسْجِدَ** is a useful beginner *direct-object construction*, but do not say **دَخَلَ** never takes any preposition. The Urdu calls that construction **بالواسطہ** despite explaining “without a preposition,” reversing the grammatical point. The text also introduces **مَشَى** and **بَعْدَ يَوْمَيْنِ** without using them in the core outcome; remove or gloss them as background.
5. **Lesson 4 again teaches Chapter 2's position-word list, with false explanations and an unrelated reveal.** **بَيْنَ** is not derived from a root meaning “two,” nor is it *always* followed by **وَ**. Position words in these constructions should not be presented as identical to prepositions; a simple “the following noun is genitive here” is enough. The hook/reveal is [Al-Falaq 113:2](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=113&verse=2), **مِن شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ**; none of **أَمَامَ، خَلْفَ، فَوْقَ، تَحْتَ، بَيْنَ** occurs there. Its explanation imports **خَلْف** into an invented dawn image. Replace with a real target or use a clearly marked authored scene.
6. **Review and language quality.** The current six-question review devotes two questions to matching old word lists, repeats the direct-object Urdu error, and has no distinct `assessment` final test. Arabic vocalization/transliteration and Urdu need line-by-line editing: examples include **الْأَبُ** transliterated *al-aba*, **الْمَائِدَةِ** transliterated *al-maa'idi*, awkward **الْفُلَانِيُّ**, and untranslated English such as “construction,” “spatial relationship,” and “toolkit.” The map names `reader_lecture_21_places_movement.md`, while fixtures name several `reader_lecture_21_*.md` files; none was found locally. Confirm provenance rather than inventing a source.

Schema validation cannot catch these semantic or Quran-target errors. Verify the displayed Quran word, its verse reference, translation, zero-based highlight index, and actual learning question as one unit.

This is **four teaching lessons, not an added fifth vocabulary lesson**. The two additions are justified by distinct product roles: CL8 for multi-turn use and a final test for mastery. The conversation lab should follow teaching and precede review/test, as the Conversation Labs roadmap requires.

### CL8 sketch for editorial review

The following is an **authored practice scene, not Quran text**. It uses the familiar **أَيْنَ؟** question, the origin–destination language taught in Lessons 1–2, and the **بَيْنَ ... وَ ...** relation taught in Lesson 3. Confirm final Arabic, vocalization, translation, and naturalness with an Arabic editor:

> A: أَيْنَ أَحْمَدُ؟  
> B: خَرَجَ مِنَ الْمَدْرَسَةِ وَذَهَبَ إِلَى الْمَسْجِدِ.  
> A: وَأَيْنَ الْمَسْجِدُ؟  
> B: الْمَسْجِدُ بَيْنَ الْمَدْرَسَةِ وَالسُّوقِ.

This is a **landmark-and-journey exchange**, not a promise that the learner can give walking instructions. The revised scene should also be reflected in the Conversation Labs roadmap after approval; leaving its old “Expanded Directions” phrase list unchanged would create two conflicting CL8 specifications. If the owner expects actual turn-by-turn directions, defer that outcome until a later chapter teaches suitable imperative forms and direction vocabulary; do not smuggle them into CL8.

### Practice and final-test blueprint

Favor questions with a visible reason for the answer: select the origin/destination from a short sentence; answer **مِنْ أَيْنَ؟ / إِلَى أَيْنَ؟** from supplied text; distinguish “entered the mosque” from “left the mosque”; locate a destination between two named landmarks; supply a relevant reply to **أَيْنَ؟**; or identify **فَخَرَجَ** in an actual ayah. An optional illustration may help, but the assessment must remain answerable from text if the present lesson UI has no map interaction. Every distractor must be grammatical and plausibly wrong for the *taught distinction*. Do not make a word's gender, case ending, or a verse number the hidden deciding clue.

| Final-test outcome | Questions |
|---|---:|
| Identify origin and destination in a short journey | 3 |
| Distinguish **ذَهَبَ / خَرَجَ / دَخَلَ / رَجَعَ** in context | 3 |
| Locate a place using a landmark or answer **أَيْنَ؟** | 2 |
| Understand the supported journey-plus-landmark exchange | 2 |
| Read one verified Quran movement target with supplied context | 2 |
| **Total** | **12** |

Confirm 9/12 fails and 10/12 passes; retries must not duplicate rewards. The lab should assess comprehension and structured response selection, **not pronunciation quality**.
