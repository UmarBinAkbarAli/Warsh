# Warsh Conversation Labs — Curriculum Proposal

**Status:** Proposed for product-owner, curriculum, and scholarly review  
**Scope:** Curriculum design only; this proposal does not authorize fixture, database, application, audio, or production changes  
**Course:** Existing 72-chapter Quran-first Fusha Arabic path  
**Recommended model:** Recurring `SPOKEN_PHRASES` lessons inside selected chapters, not additional chapters or a separate Speak tab

**Planning point (2026-09-23):** The chapter-by-chapter content revision has reached Chapter 14. Labs in Chapters 3–11 are a proposed retrofit to already reviewed chapters; labs in Chapters 15–72 are a forward roadmap to design as those chapters are reviewed. Chapter 12 already has introduction and halaqa content that should be refined during a separate follow-up.

## 1. Recommendation

Warsh should add a structured **Conversation Lab path** across the existing 72 chapters.

The labs should not turn Warsh into a dialect-conversation course. Their purpose is to help learners use the Quranic and Fusha Arabic they have already learned in coherent, respectful, practical exchanges.

Each lab should:

- remain inside the chapter where its grammar becomes usable;
- use only previously taught grammar for assessed production;
- introduce a small amount of scene vocabulary through listening and recognition;
- contain one coherent conversation rather than unrelated alternating phrases;
- move from listening to guided response and then limited production;
- include English and Urdu guidance;
- use the existing listen, record, compare, and retry speaking experience; and
- appear before the chapter review and final test.

Adding separate conversation chapters is not recommended at this stage because the 72-chapter path is already a locked product structure. Conversation should become a recurring application layer within that path.

These lessons should be labeled and assessed accordingly instead of being presented as ordinary dialogue.

## 3. Progression model

### Early foundation — CL1 to CL6 (Chapters 3–11)

Learners listen, identify meaning, select a natural reply, and shadow short lines. Production is limited to familiar two-to-four-word responses.

### Guided exchange — CL7 to CL10

Learners order short dialogues, choose context-appropriate replies, and complete four-to-six-line exchanges.

### Connected Fusha — CL11 to CL15

Learners use present-tense verbs, time expressions, polite requests, and linked responses in six-to-eight-line scenarios.

### Applied fluency — CL16 to CL18

Learners complete longer practical scenes, transfer known patterns to new details, and perform a final integrated exchange with reduced support.

### Placement criteria for every chapter

Place a lab where its main question, answer pattern, and vocabulary have already been taught. Prefer the earliest chapter that can support a useful exchange; revisit the same function later with richer grammar. In order of importance, check:

1. **Grammar readiness:** Can the learner form the scored response using structures already taught?
2. **Vocabulary readiness:** Are most content words known, with only a few new scene words?
3. **Assessment fairness:** Is an unfamiliar verb form or address form restricted to listening/recognition?
4. **Chapter fit:** Does the dialogue apply that chapter's actual learning objectives?
5. **Usefulness and coherence:** Does it solve one real communication task through a natural exchange?
6. **Load and progress:** Is there room in the chapter, and can stable IDs and completed progress be preserved?
7. **Quran-first Fusha fit:** Does it strengthen formal Arabic without treating Quran or devotional wording as fictional dialogue?

For the early labs, target roughly 70–80% already taught productive language. The remaining words may be introduced as scene vocabulary or fixed recognition phrases. This is a design check, not a rigid percentage gate; every assessed item still needs a clear teaching source.

## 4. Standard Conversation Lab structure

Every lab should use the same predictable learning sequence.

1. **Scene and mission** — where the learner is and what they need to accomplish.
2. **Listen first** — hear the complete exchange once without interruption.
3. **Phrase discovery** — learn a small, level-appropriate phrase set with context, translation, and audio.
4. **Notice the pattern** — one brief connection to previously taught grammar.
5. **Choose the response** — select the most natural reply.
6. **Build the line** — arrange familiar words into a response.
7. **Order the exchange** — reconstruct the conversation.
8. **Complete the missing turn** — infer a line from the surrounding dialogue.
9. **Shadow practice** — listen, record, compare, and retry 3–5 key lines.
10. **Conversation mission** — complete a short role-play with reduced guidance.
11. **Close** — state what the learner can now do in Fusha.

Recommended lesson size:

- 8–12 phrases where the chapter supports them;
- one coherent 4–6-turn dialogue in early labs, growing to 6–10 turns later;
- 6–9 comprehension and construction exercises;
- 3–5 shadow-repeat targets;
- one final conversation mission; and
- about 8–12 minutes for early labs and 12–15 minutes for later labs.

## 5. Detailed lesson plans

### CL1 — Greetings and First Meeting

**Host:** Chapter 3  
**Action:** Rebuild the existing `SPOKEN_PHRASES` greeting lesson, display lesson 6 (`ch03-l06`), while retaining its stable lesson ID  
**Fixture note:** the greeting lesson is `chapter-03-lesson-06-idafa-usage.json`. Despite its filename, `chapter-03-lesson-05-spoken-phrases.json` is a `STANDARD` lesson on **بِسْمِ اللَّهِ** and is not part of this lab.  
**Scene:** Two learners meet before a Quranic Arabic lesson.

#### Learner outcome

The learner can exchange a greeting, ask and answer a name, welcome the other person, and close the first meeting politely.

#### Core phrase set

- **السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ** — Peace be upon you.
- **وَعَلَيْكُمُ السَّلَامُ** — And peace be upon you.
- **مَا اسْمُكَ؟** — What is your name? (to a man)
- **مَا اسْمُكِ؟** — What is your name? (to a woman; recognition)
- **اسْمِي عُمَرُ** — My name is Umar.
- **أَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا** — Welcome.
- **مَرْحَبًا** — Hello/welcome.
- **جَزَاكَ اللَّهُ خَيْرًا** — May Allah reward you with good.

#### Model exchange

> A: السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ  
> B: وَعَلَيْكُمُ السَّلَامُ  
> A: مَا اسْمُكَ؟  
> B: اسْمِي عُمَرُ  
> A: أَهْلًا وَسَهْلًا  
> B: جَزَاكَ اللَّهُ خَيْرًا

#### Assessment mission

The learner selects and builds the appropriate response for three moments: greeting, name question, and welcome.

#### Boundaries

Do not assess nationality, profession, or extended self-description yet.

### CL2 — Where Is It?

**Host:** Chapter 5, after its location and first-action lessons  
**Action:** Add a short `SPOKEN_PHRASES` lesson before the review/test, using Chapter 2's **أَيْنَ، فِي، عَلَى، تَحْتَ**  
**Scene:** Two learners look for a book and pen before class.

#### Learner outcome

The learner can ask where a familiar object is and answer with a familiar place expression. This is a location exchange, not full route giving.

#### Core phrase set

- **أَيْنَ الْكِتَابُ؟** — Where is the book?
- **الْكِتَابُ فِي الْبَيْتِ** — The book is in the house.
- **الْكِتَابُ عَلَى الْمَكْتَبِ** — The book is on the desk.
- **أَيْنَ الْقَلَمُ؟** — Where is the pen?
- **الْقَلَمُ عَلَى الْمَكْتَبِ** — The pen is on the desk.
- **الْقَلَمُ تَحْتَ الْكِتَابِ** — The pen is under the book.
- **هَلْ هَذَا هُوَ الْكِتَابُ؟** — Is this the book? (recognition only if **هَلْ / هُوَ** have not been taught productively)
- **نَعَمْ، هَذَا هُوَ** — Yes, this is it. (recognition only)

#### Model exchange

> A: أَيْنَ الْكِتَابُ؟  
> B: الْكِتَابُ عَلَى الْمَكْتَبِ  
> A: وَأَيْنَ الْقَلَمُ؟  
> B: الْقَلَمُ تَحْتَ الْكِتَابِ

#### Assessment mission

Choose and build two **أَيْنَ؟** responses using **فِي** or **عَلَى**; use **تَحْتَ** only if its Chapter 2 teaching and audio are confirmed in the staging build.

#### Boundaries

Do not assess imperatives, street directions, or new movement verbs. The Chapter 5 form **ذَهَبَ** may appear in contextual listening, but this lab's production target is location.

### CL5 — Meet Your Study Group

**Host:** Chapter 10, after its independent plural pronouns  
**Action:** Add one `SPOKEN_PHRASES` lesson before the review/test  
**Scene:** Learners meet other students before a lesson and ask a teacher for repetition.

#### Learner outcome

The learner can identify their own and another group using **نَحْنُ، هُمْ، هُنَّ** and recognise useful fixed classroom replies.

#### Core phrase set

- **السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ** — Peace be upon you.
- **وَعَلَيْكُمُ السَّلَامُ** — And peace be upon you.
- **نَحْنُ طُلَّابٌ** — We are students.
- **هُمْ طُلَّابٌ** — They are students (male or mixed group).
- **هُنَّ طَالِبَاتٌ** — They are female students.
- **أَنْتُمْ طُلَّابٌ** — You all are students (male or mixed group).
- **هَلْ فَهِمْتُمْ؟** — Did you all understand? (fixed phrase, listening/recognition only)
- **نَعَمْ، فَهِمْنَا** — Yes, we understood. (fixed phrase, listening/recognition only)
- **مَرَّةً أُخْرَى، لَوْ سَمَحْتَ** — Once again, please. (fixed phrase, listening/recognition only)

#### Model exchange

> A: السَّلَامُ عَلَيْكُمْ  
> B: وَعَلَيْكُمُ السَّلَامُ  
> A: أَنْتُمْ طُلَّابٌ؟  
> B: نَعَمْ، نَحْنُ طُلَّابٌ  
> A: وَهُمْ؟  
> B: هُمْ طُلَّابٌ

#### Assessment mission

Choose **نَحْنُ، هُمْ، هُنَّ** or **أَنْتُمْ** for a pictured group; recognise the appropriate response when the teacher asks whether the group understood.

#### Boundaries

Assess the taught pronoun-plus-noun sentences. Treat **فَهِمْتُمْ، فَهِمْنَا** and **لَوْ سَمَحْتَ** as whole listening phrases only; do not score conjugation, derivation, or free production of them. Do not assess origin or profession before Chapter 12.

### CL6 — Home and Family

**Host:** Chapter 11  
**Action:** Add one `SPOKEN_PHRASES` lesson before the review and chapter test  
**Scene:** A learner shows a family photograph and describes who is at home.

#### Learner outcome

The learner can identify immediate family members and state where a person is.

#### Core phrase set

- **مَنْ هَذَا؟** — Who is this?
- **هَذَا أَبِي** — This is my father.
- **مَنْ هَذِهِ؟** — Who is this?
- **هَذِهِ أُمِّي** — This is my mother.
- **هَذَا أَخِي** — This is my brother.
- **هَذِهِ أُخْتِي** — This is my sister.
- **أَيْنَ أُمُّكَ؟** — Where is your mother? (to a man; heard question, recognition only)
- **أُمِّي فِي الْبَيْتِ** — My mother is in the house.
- **أَيْنَ أُخْتُكَ؟** — Where is your sister? (to a man; heard question, recognition only)
- **أُخْتِي فِي الْغُرْفَةِ** — My sister is in the room.

#### Model exchange

> A: مَنْ هَذَا؟  
> B: هَذَا أَبِي  
> A: وَمَنْ هَذِهِ؟  
> B: هَذِهِ أُمِّي  
> A: أَيْنَ أُخْتُكَ؟  
> B: أُخْتِي فِي الْبَيْتِ

#### Assessment mission

Identify two family members and answer one location question.

#### Boundaries

Do not introduce family counts or number agreement. Address-form variation should remain recognition-level unless already taught.

Chapter 11 teaches the learner's own family words (**أَبِي، أُمِّي، أَخِي، أُخْتِي**) but not the "your" forms. **أُمُّكَ** and **أُخْتُكَ** combine taught parts (the noun and Chapter 7's **كَ**) regularly, so they may appear as heard questions; only the **ـِي** answers are scored. Exclude **أَبُوكَ / أَخُوكَ**: these belong to the five nouns (الأسماء الخمسة), whose long vowel is not taught by Chapter 11.

#### Boundaries

#### Boundaries

### CL9 — Ask, Answer, and Confirm Understanding

- Heard only: **لَا، لَمْ أَفْهَمْ** (No, I did not understand), **هَلْ فَهِمْتِ الدَّرْسَ؟** (to a woman), **هَلْ فَهِمْتُمْ؟** (to a group).

#### Model exchange

> A: هَلْ فَهِمْتَ الدَّرْسَ؟
> B: نَعَمْ، فَهِمْتُ الدَّرْسَ
> A: مَاذَا قَالَ الْأُسْتَاذُ؟
> B: قَالَ الْأُسْتَاذُ: الدَّرْسُ غَدًا
> A: أَيْنَ الدَّرْسُ؟
> B: الدَّرْسُ فِي الْمَسْجِدِ

The learner's own lines are the same for male and female learners; the gendered question forms are heard only.

#### Assessment mission

The teacher asks: request repetition, report what he said, and say where tomorrow's lesson is.

#### Boundaries

Do not assess **لَمْ** before its dedicated chapter; the negative reply stays heard only.

## 7. Content rules

### Fusha boundary

- Use Quran-compatible Fusha and broadly understood formal Arabic.
- Do not introduce Pakistani, Gulf, Egyptian, Levantine, or other dialect forms as the course answer.
- A common modern Fusha expression may be used when it is appropriate to the scene and clearly reviewed.

### Grammar boundary

- Production exercises may use only previously taught grammar.
- A useful phrase containing later grammar may appear for listening and recognition only, clearly labeled as a whole phrase.
- No lab should become a hidden grammar lesson.

### Quran-first boundary

- Recycle Quranic roots and vocabulary where natural.
- Do not turn Quran verses into artificial replies between fictional speakers.
- Quran recitation, duʿa, talbiyah, adhan response, and khutbah listening should be preserved as their own authentic modes rather than mislabeled as everyday conversation.

### Address and gender

- Teach one productive address form at the earliest level and expose the counterpart through recognition.
- Expand to productive masculine/feminine address only after the required forms have been taught.
- Explanations must identify who is speaking and who is being addressed.

### Audio and speaking

- Every phrase and every complete dialogue needs verified Fusha audio.
- A changed Arabic string requires regenerated audio because lesson audio is text-hash keyed.
- Speaker turns should use distinguishable voices when practical.
- The app may support record, compare, and retry; it must not claim that pronunciation was automatically graded.

## 10. Acceptance criteria for every lab

- [ ] The scene has one clear practical mission.
- [ ] The dialogue is coherent and contextually natural.
- [ ] Every response answers the preceding turn.
- [ ] Assessed grammar was taught before or within the host chapter.
- [ ] Recognition-only structures are clearly marked and are not scored as production.
- [ ] English and Urdu express the same conversational purpose.
- [ ] Arabic, transliteration, and translation have been editorially reviewed.
- [ ] No dialect form is required as the correct answer.
- [ ] Quranic or devotional material is used authentically.
- [ ] Every phrase and dialogue line has verified audio.
- [ ] Exercises progress from listening to guided construction.
- [ ] The final mission requires meaningful comprehension, not memorized position.
- [ ] Speaking practice makes no automated pronunciation-grading claim.
- [ ] Stable IDs and completed learner progress are preserved or safely backfilled.
- [ ] The chapter review and final test remain distinct and correctly locked.
- [ ] Isolated staging review passes before any scoped production promotion.
