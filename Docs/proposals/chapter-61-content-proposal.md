# Chapter 61 — Trade, Weights, and Practical Shopping: Content Proposal

**Status:** Proposal only; not approved or implemented  
**Scope:** Chapter 61 map and learner-facing content after owner approval; instrument-morphology placement requires a cross-chapter decision  
**Current sources:** `warsh-backend/prisma/curriculum-books7-8.cjs` and `warsh-backend/prisma/fixtures/chapter-61-lesson-01.json` through `chapter-61-lesson-07.json`

## Recommendation

Keep the Book 7 theme of honest trade, measurement, and shopping language, but rebuild the chapter around practical Arabic a learner can understand and use. Remove invented or incorrectly analyzed instrument nouns and the weak-verb derivation lesson. The existing unit has multiple high-confidence morphology and vocabulary errors, map/fixture drift, and no chapter completion test.

There is also a course-sequencing decision to resolve: Chapter 61 teaches **اسم الآلة** as a formal topic, but Chapter 65's completion message says that morphology begins in Book 8, while Chapter 66 currently begins with **ظرف الزمان والمكان**. This proposal recommends keeping Chapter 61 focused on commerce and measurement; it may use a few everyday tool words as vocabulary, but should not teach the morphology system here. Before implementation, the curriculum owner must reconcile Chapter 65's Book 7/8 handoff and the Chapter 66 plan. Do not simply move Chapter 61's current broken lessons into Chapter 66 and create a duplication with its existing topic.

## Continuity and placement decision

1. Chapter 60 is proposed as practical travel/Hajj Arabic, and Chapter 61 can extend that applied approach into market language. Reuse only the travel words needed for a specific exchange; do not repeat Chapter 60's full travel lexicon.
2. Keep the trade/measure moral context tied to what 83:1–3 actually says. Chapter 61's Qur'an practice should support vocabulary comprehension rather than launch a new, unprepared morphology block.
3. The current Chapter 65 close is evidence of the intended book-level promise, but it is itself only current fixture content, not proof that the boundary is pedagogically correct. Chapter 66 currently has six zarf lessons and a review. The next Chapter 66 audit must decide whether the Book 8 opener is actually zarf, instrument morphology, or an approved spiral sequence, and coordinate any move with Chapter 61.
4. Chapter 61 should not call learners experts in “Islamic commerce” or promise they can understand Quranic instrument nouns after presenting invalid forms. Its outcomes should be bounded to words, quantities, and exchanges actually practiced.

## Proposed lesson sequence

Four core lessons plus a formative review and a distinct final test are recommended. This gives the trade and measurement outcomes a meaningful application sequence without padding the chapter with speculative derivations.

1. **Trade words in context** (`STANDARD`, rebuild). Teach a small, high-frequency set (merchant, item/goods, buying/selling, price, fair/honest) with checked singulars, plurals, verb forms, and collocations. Use **أَمِينٌ** only with an example whose meaning is clear; keep moral claims tied to source text.
2. **Weight, volume, amount, and total** (`STANDARD`, rebuild). Contrast weighing and volume measurement, and add practical quantities and a simple total-price expression after the number forms are checked. Use accurate examples from ordinary transactions; connect to **وَأَوْفُوا الْكَيْلَ... وَزِنُوا بِالْقِسْطَاسِ الْمُسْتَقِيمِ** (17:35) or the relevant phrase in 83:3 as supported reading, not as a modern unit chart.
3. **At the market: a multi-turn shopping dialogue** (`STANDARD`, rebuild). Practice greeting, asking for an item and price, asking about a discount politely, confirming amount/total, accepting or declining, and closing. Provide at least two situations, comprehensible response choices, natural audio, and one clarification/repair turn.
4. **Short reading: fair measure and clear exchange** (`STANDARD`, rebuild). Read a short, fully referenced passage—preferably 83:1–3, whose words actually discuss giving less/full measure—or an authored marketplace dialogue. Focus on meaning and the commercial language already taught. Do not make **اسم الآلة** a prerequisite or test outcome here.
5. **Trade and measurement retrieval review** (`REVIEW`, new/rebuilt). Formative mixed recall across taught words, quantity/measure distinctions, and the dialogue. Feedback should explain vocabulary and response fit; it must not reinforce “unrealistic price” distractors or invented derivatives.
6. **Chapter 61 final test** (`REVIEW`, new). Distinct `CHAPTER_TEST` covering the approved vocabulary and communicative outcomes. Use a documented blueprint that maps questions to instruction; include a short comprehension item and contextual dialogue response. Keep unsupported morphology and legal/religious rulings out of scoring.

If the approved level warrants more guided speaking or listening practice, split the dialogue into two scaffolded lessons (first controlled turns, then integrated role-play). Add depth only where it closes a demonstrated learning gap; don't restore the current weak-root derivation content to reach a preferred lesson count.

## Assessment, review, and acceptance criteria

Before authoring the test, specify each question's taught outcome, source lesson, accepted answer(s), distractor rationale, and point value. Avoid grading open voice recordings; use authored/selectable or self-practice speaking and assess the learner's comprehension through supported formats. Provide accurate English/Urdu feedback. The final lesson must be a distinct `REVIEW` with the canonical `CHAPTER_TEST` payload; the formative review must not stand in for it.

Have a qualified Arabic reviewer verify every lexical meaning, root/form/pattern claim, number, case ending, question, and dialogue; verify Quran text, references, translations, and highlights against a reliable Quran source. Have English and Urdu reviewed independently. Any religious interpretation should be reviewed by a qualified subject-matter reviewer and marked as interpretation where appropriate.

Chapter 61 is ready to implement only when:

- the curriculum owner has resolved the Book 7/8 and Chapter 61/66 instrument-morphology placement;
- map and fixtures have matching approved outcomes, titles, sources, and order;
- invented **اسم الآلة** forms and false root stories are removed, and instrument morphology is taught only in the approved location with reviewed evidence;
- shopping and weighing/volume exchanges use natural, level-appropriate MSA and clear, non-subjective exercise feedback;
- Quran anchors quote the correct passage and directly support the selected learning objective;
- a formative review and separate final `CHAPTER_TEST` exist and chapter completion follows the product contract;
- English, Urdu, Arabic, transliteration, audio, choices, answer keys, and reveal copy have all been reviewed.

After approval and implementation, validate with the lesson schema, Quran-text audit, Urdu audit, and assessment/backend completion tests. Run `npm run content:check` before any fixture sync. This proposal authorizes no fixture edits, database writes, content synchronization, or publication.
