# Chapter 57 — Weak Verbs and the Five Imperfect Forms

**Status:** Proposal only — not approved or implemented  
**Evidence reviewed:** Active product/technical specifications; the Chapter 57 curriculum-map entry and all ten Chapter 57 fixtures (including the spoken-phrases lesson); Chapter 56’s map, fixtures, and proposal; and Chapter 58–59 map entries for forward scope. Quran text and selected grammar claims were checked against the Quranic Arabic Corpus and university/academic grammar references linked below. No fixture, database, or curriculum-map content has been changed.

## Recommendation

Rebuild Chapter 57 as two connected but explicitly distinct strands: (1) common weak-verb patterns and (2) the five inflected imperfect forms, **الأفعال الخمسة**. Learners should first understand which root radicals are weak and how the common hollow/final-weak patterns change; then learn the five imperfect forms and how retention/deletion of **ن** marks mood. Keep **كان وأخواتها** as a short syntax application after reviewing the correct rule: **اسم كان** is nominative and **خبر كان** is accusative.

The current chapter contains repeated foundational errors, including calling **آمَنَ** weak because it begins with alif, treating **اسم كان** as accusative, teaching both **اسم كان** and **خبر كان** as accusative, and defining the five imperfect forms as indeclinable. It also calls imperatives members of **الأفعال الخمسة**, misattributes an **أَن تُقَاتِلُوا** example to a verse that does not contain it, and repeats Chapter 56’s inaccurate five-noun rule in its review. These errors are repeated across concept cards, exercise feedback, Quran annotations, the curriculum map, and the Khutbah spoken lesson; correcting only the headline explanations would leave the learner-facing mistakes intact.

Use **eight core instructional lessons**, retain a revised spoken-practice lesson as applied listening/shadowing (not as evidence of full grammar mastery), add a formative mixed `REVIEW`, and add a separate `REVIEW` with the canonical `CHAPTER_TEST` assessment payload. Chapter 56’s misplaced five-verbs lesson should be removed as proposed there; Chapter 57 should be the single home for this verb topic. Chapter 57 should not try to teach every weak-verb subtype or all of **كان وأخواتها**: select a few high-frequency patterns and label the boundary clearly.

## Continuity and boundaries

- **Chapter 55** introduces nominal iʿrāb and distinguishes case from imperfect-verb mood. That proposal is not yet approved; confirm actual prerequisite coverage before relying on it. Give a brief case-versus-mood retrieval prompt if needed.
- **Chapter 56** is intended for special noun forms. Its current Lesson 9 duplicates and mis-teaches **الأفعال الخمسة**; do not treat that fixture as a valid prerequisite. Chapter 57 must define and teach the topic correctly from the beginning.
- **Chapter 57** owns the common weak-verb patterns, a bounded **كان وأخواتها** application, and the five imperfect forms with their rafʿ/naṣb/jazm behavior. Keep root weakness (morphology) separate from mood (syntax/inflection), even when one example illustrates both.
- **Chapter 58** owns higher-level syntax. Do not turn Chapter 57 into an exhaustive treatment of all particles, advanced conditional syntax, every sister of **كان**, or full Quran parsing.
- **Chapter 59** is the Book 6 capstone. Chapter 57 practice should prepare for later integration, not claim mastery of “all Quranic grammar” or a complete grammatical toolkit.
- The existing Khutbah phrase lesson can remain as useful listening/shadowing if the Quran text, grammar sidebars, and audio are verified. It should not imply that a learner can already parse every phrase, and the setting must not be framed as call-and-response during an active sermon. Keep it distinct from the core verb-rule sequence and chapter test blueprint.

## Proposed lesson plan

1. **Weak radicals: what counts and what changes** (`STANDARD`, rebuilt): Introduce **صحيح** versus **معتل** roots. Use **ق-و-ل، ك-و-ن، ر-م-ي** as selected weak examples and **ء-م-ن، ك-ف-ر** as strong contrasts. Explain that alif can represent a weak radical in a form but hamza is not itself a weak radical. Avoid frequency claims without corpus evidence.
2. **Hollow verbs in common past forms** (`STANDARD`, revised): Teach **أجوف** with **قَالَ / قُلْتُ / يَقُولُ** and **كَانَ / كُنْتُ / يَكُونُ**. Show exact stem changes without claiming wāw turns into yāʾ. Keep person suffixes and weak-root behavior visually distinct.
3. **Final-weak verbs across useful forms** (`STANDARD`, rebuilt): Choose one final-weak verb such as **رَمَى / يَرْمِي** or **دَعَا / يَدْعُو**, and show only the high-frequency forms needed for course reading. Explain its root and changes with reviewed morphology; do not call every alif a root radical.
4. **كَانَ and its sisters: verb pattern plus case effect** (`STANDARD`, substantially revised): Teach a small set of sisters and the accurate pairing **اسم كان مرفوع / خبر كان منصوب**. Connect **كَانَ** to its weak root only after the syntax is clear. Defer sisters with additional conditions and complex predicate types.
5. **The five imperfect forms: recognize the patterns** (`STANDARD`, revised): Present the five person/number patterns, clearly distinguish them from the ordinary singular **يَفْعَلُ**, and name the attached subject pronouns. State that they are imperfect verbs and **muʿrab**, not mabnī.
6. **Nūn retained or deleted: rafʿ, naṣb, and jazm** (`VERB_PATTERN`, rebuilt): Show the same forms under no governor, **أَنْ / لَنْ**, and **لَمْ / لا الناهية**. Pair form and particle to teach when nūn remains/deletes; distinguish naṣb from jazm by the trigger. Use one paradigm at a time before the full five-row table.
7. **Imperatives are related—but not one of the five forms** (`STANDARD`, new/reworked): Brief contrast with a plural imperative such as **اتَّقُوا**: explain its form separately and note that the nūn deletion sign is shared but its grammatical category is different. Do not teach imperative as “a مجزوم form.” If this lesson proves redundant after the main five-form lesson, use it as a short contrast card within Lesson 6 instead.
8. **Weak verbs and the five forms in a short Quranic reading** (`STANDARD`, rebuilt): Apply only the taught outcomes to verified, short excerpts. Separate root classification from mood and parse. For example, use **يُؤْمِنُونَ** as a five-form example, not as a weak-root example; use a genuinely weak verb for weak morphology. Do not attempt an exhaustive parse of a verse whose particles/structures are not yet taught.
9. **Khutbah listening and shadowing** (`SPOKEN_PHRASES`, revised): Retain the applied speech lesson if audio and phrase text are confirmed, but frame it as listening and shadowing, not grammar mastery or interactive khutbah dialogue. Remove or correct the inaccurate grammar sidebars, and do not let this lesson carry the chapter's assessment outcomes.
10. **Chapter 57 formative retrieval** (`REVIEW`, revised): Mixed retrieval across weak-root recognition, the selected weak paradigms, **كان** case roles, five-form identification, and mood triggers. Include explanatory feedback and use only corrected examples. This is not the completion test.
11. **Chapter 57 checkpoint** (`REVIEW`, new): A distinct schema-valid `CHAPTER_TEST` assessment mapped to the stated outcomes, following canonical grading and completion behavior. Do not invent a pass threshold or test length here.
