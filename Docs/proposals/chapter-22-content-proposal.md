# Chapter 22 — Dialogue and Communication: content repair proposal

**Status:** Approved with the review amendments in section 7 and implemented 2026-09-24  
**Scope:** Chapter 22 map, five existing fixtures/seed rows, one proposed Conversation Lab, and one proposed final test  
**Recommended structure:** Four teaching lessons, one Conversation Lab, one review, one distinct final test (seven items)

## 1. Decision and learner outcome

Keep Chapter 22 about **following a short exchange** rather than memorizing three verb tables. A learner should finish able to identify **who spoke, what was said, who asked, what answer addressed the question, and whether the listener understood**. The chapter should support one practical classroom conversation without claiming to teach all Arabic dialogue or a full past-tense conjugation paradigm.

Specific outcomes:

1. Find the speaker and spoken words after **قَالَ / قَالَتْ**, including a verified Quran example.
2. Identify a question using familiar **هَلْ، أَيْنَ، مَا** and understand **سَأَلَ / سَأَلَتْ** in context. A question may also be introduced by **قَالَ**; do not teach one-verb/one-sentence-type rules.
3. Recognize **أَجَابَ / أَجَابَتْ** as “answered” and select a reply that actually addresses the displayed question. A **نَعَمْ** or **لَا** answer requires supplied facts or a clearly stated speaker identity to be uniquely gradable.
4. Use the small **هَلْ فَهِمْتَ الدَّرْسَ؟ / نَعَمْ، فَهِمْتُ** exchange as a *taught, fixed classroom phrase set*; do not derive all second-/first-person past endings from it. Offer a polite request for repetition as a supported phrase, not a new imperative-formation lesson.
5. Follow and complete a coherent multi-turn classroom conversation, then pass a separate chapter final test.

The Quran is a source of authentic **speech frames**, not evidence that every verse contains **سَأَلَ** and **أَجَابَ** or that every dialogue has the same three-verb sequence. Authored practice conversations must be labeled as authored, never attributed to an ayah.

## 3. Findings requiring repair

1. **The Quran reveals misidentify words and roles.** `ch22-l01` says [Yusuf 12:36](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=12&verse=36) contains **وَقَالَ لَهُ** and that Yusuf's brothers speak to him. The actual verse describes two young men entering prison and says **قَالَ أَحَدُهُمَا** (“one of them said”); its zero-based reveal index `1` does not point to **قَالَ**. `ch22-l02` treats [Yusuf 12:77](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=12&verse=77) as an explicit “Did he steal?” question and a **سَأَلَ** example; it begins **قَالُوا إِن يَسْرِقْ**, not that question or the verb **سَأَلَ**. `ch22-l03` calls [Al-Baqarah 2:111](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=2&verse=111) an answer after a displayed question; it begins **وَقَالُوا** and does not show **أَجَابَ**. The current map's [2:83](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=2&verse=83) genuinely contains **وَقُولُوا لِلنَّاسِ حُسْنًا**, but **قُولُوا** is an imperative (“speak/say”), not **قَالَ** (“he said”). Any retained excerpt needs exact context and a recognition-only explanation of the different form.
2. **The central grammar rule contradicts the examples.** Current cards state or imply **قَالَ = statement**, **سَأَلَ = question**, **أَجَابَ = answer**, and that **أَجَابَ** always follows **سَأَلَ**. A speaker can **say** a question; `ch22-l04` itself uses **قَالَ الطَّالِبُ: هَلْ ...؟**. Teach the meanings of the verbs and the *function of the quoted utterance* separately. **أَيْنَ** is a question word, not a generic “interrogative particle.” Remove “all dialogue follows this sequence” and “now you can read [all of] Surah Yusuf” claims.
3. **Plural/dual examples are unsafe as beginner models.** `ch22-l01` uses **قَالُوا الْوَلَدُونَ**, and exercises use **قَالُوا الرِّجَالُ**, **قَالُوا الْأَصْدِقَاءُ**, **سَأَلُوا الْوَلَدُونَ**, and **أَجَابُوا الرِّجَالُ** as if a preceding plural verb plus an overt plural subject were the ordinary beginner pattern. Replace with editor-approved simple **قَالَ الرِّجَالُ** (verb before named plural subject) or **الرِّجَالُ قَالُوا** (named subject before plural verb), according to the intended lesson. **الْوَلَدُونَ** is not a good ordinary plural for “boys.” The six-form tables label **قَالَتَا / سَأَلَتَا / أَجَابَتَا** as “they-f,” concealing that these are *dual*, not feminine plural; remove the tables unless the chapter genuinely teaches and tests their distinctions.
4. **Several answer keys are ungradeable or impossible.** In `ch22-l02-ex06`, both “Yes, I am a student” and “No, I am a teacher” are valid responses to **هَلْ أَنْتَ طَالِبٌ؟** without speaker context. `ch22-l03-ex06` has the same yes/no ambiguity for “Is the mosque near?”; `ch22-l04-ex08` repeats it for “Are you a student?” `ch22-l04-ex05` asks learners to build “Is the mosque far? No, it is near,” but its tiles include **أَيْنَ** and **بَعِيدٌ** and cannot build the target. Matching sets such as `ch22-l01-ex04` and `ch22-l04-ex04` duplicate identical right-column answers (“she said” or “statement”), so the allegedly unique pairs are ambiguous. Rewrite prompts, tiles, distractors, keys, and feedback together; do not merely change `correct_index`.
5. **Lesson 4 and review repeat glosses without a real conversation skill.** A short, contextual dialogue appears, but the assessment mostly asks learners to match **قَالَ / سَأَلَ / أَجَابَ** again. `ch22-l05` has only six exercises, no coherent comprehension/repair scenario, and no distinct final test. Its [Al-Kafirun 109:1](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=109&verse=1) hook has **قُلْ** (“Say!”); it must not imply that recognizing **قُلْ** means knowing every form of **قَالَ**.
6. **Arabic, Urdu, audio, and provenance need an editorial pass.** Some Urdu options retain “school,” “specifically,” or malformed text; some translations call a masculine **مَسْجِد** feminine. English distractors sometimes differ only between “said” and “asked,” although both can describe an uttered question in ordinary English. Check vocalization, transliteration, punctuation, speaker gender, and every quoted sentence. The fixtures' `reader_lecture_22_dialogue.md` and the map's `reader_lecture_22_dialogue_communication.md` were not found locally; confirm source metadata rather than inventing a lecture file.

## 4. Proposed seven-item sequence

Keep existing stable IDs `ch22-l01`–`ch22-l05`. Rewrite the first four `STANDARD` lessons and keep `ch22-l05` as the `REVIEW`, now displayed after a new `ch22-cl09` lab. Add `ch22-test` last. This preserves IDs and existing progress references, but changing the actual teaching targets requires an owner decision on revisits for already-completed lessons.

| Order / ID | Template | Working lesson | New job and anchor |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 / `ch22-l01` | `STANDARD` | **Who said what?** | Read **قَالَ أَحَدُهُمَا** in [Yusuf 12:36](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=12&verse=36), identify one of the two young men as speaker, and distinguish the quoted words from narration. **قَالَتْ** may appear in a short authored counterpart, retrieving the earlier feminine past marker rather than launching six-form conjugation. Verify the exact target index and context. |
| 2 / `ch22-l02` | `STANDARD` | **Who asked, and what was asked?** | Introduce **سَأَلَ / سَأَلَتْ** through one simple authored classroom question. Reuse familiar **هَلْ / أَيْنَ / مَا**; identify questioner and question. Contrast *meaning of reporting verb* with *question form*: **قَالَ** can report a question too. Do not force unrelated 12:77 into a **سَأَلَ** example. |
| 3 / `ch22-l03` | `STANDARD` | **An answer that fits** | Teach **أَجَابَ / أَجَابَتْ** in a supplied question–answer context, and use **نَعَمْ / لَا** only when a shown fact makes one reply correct. Introduce **هَلْ فَهِمْتَ الدَّرْسَ؟ / نَعَمْ، فَهِمْتُ** as fixed phrases with a male addressee in this model scene; glossary-explain their meaning without deriving a person paradigm. Add **مَرَّةً أُخْرَى، لَوْ سَمَحْتَ** as an editor-approved polite repair phrase. **لَا، لَمْ أَفْهَمْ** may be recognized in audio/context only, not scored as a new negation rule. |
| 4 / `ch22-l04` | `STANDARD` | **Follow the full exchange** | In a four-turn authored scene, identify who speaks, the question, a relevant reply, and whether repetition or confirmation is needed. Briefly compare a verified Quran **قَالَ** speech frame with this authored classroom conversation; do not attribute **سَأَلَ / أَجَابَ** to a verse lacking them. Use new comprehension questions, not a fourth matching table. |
| 5 / `ch22-cl09` | `SPOKEN_PHRASES` | **Conversation Lab 9: Ask, answer, confirm** | A learner asks about a lesson, hears an answer, and confirms understanding or politely requests repetition. Provide a model exchange, listen-and-select, turn ordering, and structured response choices. Do not claim automated pronunciation grading. Keep **لَمْ** recognition-only until its dedicated chapter. |
| 6 / `ch22-l05` | `REVIEW` | **Chapter 22 review** | Interleave speaker identification, question-versus-statement recognition, answer relevance, and understanding/repair choices in fresh mini-dialogues. No new morphology or Quran assertion. |
| 7 / `ch22-test` | `REVIEW` + `assessment` | **Chapter 22 final test** | Separate `assessment.type = CHAPTER_TEST`, 12 server-graded questions, `pass_score_percent: 80` (10/12). Preserve existing unlock, retries, and chapter-completion/reward behavior. |

The fifth item is a **conversation lab**, not an extra verb-gloss lesson. The lab must come after its phrases are taught and before review/test. If review finds Lesson 3 too dense, split *understanding/repair* into an additional teaching lesson and move lab/review/test accordingly; do not hide a new negation or full conjugation unit inside one eight-minute lesson simply to preserve a seven-item total.

### Conversation Lab 9 sketch

This is an **authored practice exchange, not Quran text**, and every Arabic/Urdu line needs human review:

> A: هَلْ فَهِمْتَ الدَّرْسَ؟  
> B: نَعَمْ، فَهِمْتُ الدَّرْسَ.  
> A: مَاذَا قَالَ الْأُسْتَاذُ؟  
> B: قَالَ الْأُسْتَاذُ: «الْكِتَابُ عَلَى الْمَكْتَبِ».

An alternate branch can ask for repetition using the *taught fixed polite phrase*; it should not score **لَمْ أَفْهَمْ** formation. The proposed phrase set uses a male addressee in **فَهِمْتَ** and **سَمَحْتَ**; an Arabic editor should decide how to support a female addressee or phrase the scene inclusively before publishing. The roadmap's CL9 phrase list should be synchronized **after approval** so there are not two conflicting CL9 specs.

### Practice and final-test blueprint

Use evidence-based tasks: point to the actual speaker in a displayed Quran excerpt; separate the narrator's **قَالَ** from the quoted utterance; choose whether a displayed line is a question; select a reply that matches a *shown fact*; identify whose understanding is being checked; choose a polite repair response; and sequence two or three turns. Make wrong answers plausible but uniquely wrong. A question such as “Are you a student?” without speaker facts cannot have only one correct yes/no response. Build-sentence tiles must be sufficient to produce the **exact stated target**; matching pairs must be uniquely identifiable.

| Final-test outcome | Questions |
|---|---:|
| Identify speaker and quoted words, including one verified Quran target | 3 |
| Identify questioner/question and distinguish reporting verb from utterance type | 2 |
| Choose an answer justified by displayed context | 3 |
| Follow an understanding/repair exchange using taught fixed phrases | 2 |
| Order or complete a coherent multi-turn classroom dialogue | 2 |
| **Total** | **12** |

Verify that 9/12 fails and 10/12 passes, and that retries do not duplicate rewards. Do not test dual verb forms, all meanings of Surah Yusuf, **لَمْ** grammar, or Chapter 28's broader **عَلِمَ / فَهِمَ** contrast.

## 7. Review amendments (owner-approved 2026-09-24) and implementation

1. **CL5 already has the repair phrases:** مَرَّةً أُخْرَى، لَوْ سَمَحْتَ and هَلْ فَهِمْتُمْ؟ / نَعَمْ، فَهِمْنَا are heard-only in Chapter 10's lab. CL9 promotes مَرَّةً أُخْرَى، لَوْ سَمَحْتَ to a spoken line instead of presenting it as new.
2. **Quran evidence for the central point:** Ali 'Imran 3:37 — قَالَ يَا مَرْيَمُ أَنَّىٰ لَكِ هَٰذَا answered by قَالَتْ هُوَ مِنْ عِندِ اللَّهِ — shows قَالَ reporting a question and قَالَتْ its answer. Al-Baqarah 2:186 وَإِذَا سَأَلَكَ عِبَادِي gives a real سَأَلَ, and Yusuf 12:90 is the verified speech frame in Lesson 4.
3. **Addressee question settled:** the learner's own lines (نَعَمْ، فَهِمْتُ الدَّرْسَ; قَالَ الْأُسْتَاذُ: …; مَرَّةً أُخْرَى، لَوْ سَمَحْتَ to the male teacher) are the same for male and female learners; هَلْ فَهِمْتِ؟ and هَلْ فَهِمْتُمْ؟ are heard only. The roadmap's CL9 entry was synchronised.
