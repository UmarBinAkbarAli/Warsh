# Chapter 31 — Common Questions in Arabic

**Status:** Proposal only — not approved or implemented
**Scope:** Curriculum content and sequencing for Chapter 31. This proposal does not edit lesson fixtures, the seed, or the database.

## Purpose

Teach learners to recognize and use a practical set of common Arabic question forms in context. The chapter should build on the learner's prior sentence and conversation skills without claiming to cover every interrogative form in Arabic or reteaching the general conversation method already introduced earlier.

### Learning outcomes

By the end of the chapter, learners should be able to:

- distinguish yes/no questions formed with **هَلْ** and the interrogative hamza from ordinary statements;
- understand the chapter's selected question words **مَنْ، مَا، أَيْنَ، كَيْفَ، مَتَى، لِمَاذَا، كَمْ**, including simple quantity questions with **كَمْ**;
- select or understand a contextually appropriate answer to a question;
- recognize that a question can ask about a nominal or verbal sentence;
- follow a short exchange that uses more than one question type; and
- use a small set of respectful, practical question phrases without mistaking language practice for religious instruction.

## Curriculum continuity and non-duplication

- **Verified fixture continuity:** Chapter 22 has an existing `SPOKEN_PHRASES` lab and review/test fixtures; Chapter 30 has an existing “Dialogue in Arabic” lesson. Chapter 31 should advance from question recognition to interpreting varied question forms and selecting relevant answers, not reteach the basic question/answer loop or generic dialogue framework. Later labs should add a distinct setting, purpose, or interaction skill.
- Earlier lessons have introduced basic sentence and verb patterns. Reuse only enough familiar language to make the question forms understandable. Do not turn this chapter into another general verb-conjugation or case lesson.
- Later conversation labs may deliberately retrieve these question forms, but should add a new communicative purpose, setting, or interaction skill rather than repeating this chapter's phrase bank. A purposeful retrieval should be brief and explicitly support the new lesson goal.
- This chapter establishes a bounded foundation for later conversation labs. Later chapters may add new settings, registers, follow-up questions, and longer exchanges; they should extend this skill rather than repeat the same phrase bank.

## Quran and Arabic content review

Quran examples need to illustrate the actual form under discussion, with exact wording, surah/ayah reference, and a learner-appropriate explanation. Initial source checks support these specific corrections:

- **At-Takwir 81:26 — فَأَيْنَ تَذْهَبُونَ** is a genuine question containing interrogative **أَيْنَ** and is suitable as a possible chapter hook after editorial review. [Quranic Arabic Corpus, 81:26](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=81&verse=26)
- In **Al-Kafirun 109:2 — لَا أَعْبُدُ مَا تَعْبُدُونَ**, **لَا** is the negator; **مَا** is not functioning as the negator in this phrase. [Quranic Arabic Corpus, 109:2](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=109&verse=2)
- **فَمَا خَطْبُكُمْ** occurs in Al-Hijr 15:57, not Yusuf 12:79. [Quranic Arabic Corpus, 15:57](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=15&verse=57)
- Yusuf 12:87 includes an imperative and a prohibition; it should not be presented as a plain present-tense yes/no-question example. [Quranic Arabic Corpus, 12:87](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=12&verse=87)

These checks support the specific textual observations above; they do not replace review by a qualified Arabic and Quran-content editor. Avoid presenting a verse's broader theological implications as a grammar lesson unless separately reviewed and sourced.

## Assessment blueprint

Use a varied, chapter-appropriate assessment rather than a set of near-identical definition questions. A 12-item blueprint could include:

- 2 items distinguishing **هَلْ**, interrogative hamza, and a statement, with tense-aware interpretation;
- 2 items matching **مَنْ، مَا، مَاذَا** to a question and contextually supported answer;
- 2 items using **أَيْنَ** and **كَيْفَ** with relevant answers;
- 2 items using **مَتَى** and **لِمَاذَا**;
- 2 items interpreting **كَمْ** in reviewed quantity examples;
- 1 item distinguishing a question about a nominal expression from one about an action; and
- 2 short dialogue/listening-comprehension items using multiple taught forms.

Distractors should be plausible but unambiguous; vary correct-answer positions. Do not assess religious rulings, unintroduced grammar, or mastery of every Arabic interrogative form. Match scoring and retries to the existing implementation rather than defining chapter-specific behavior.

## Acceptance criteria

- The chapter describes its scope as common forms, not the complete Arabic interrogative system.
- Each promised form—including **كَمْ**—has a focused, consistent explanation and at least one clear, reviewed example.
- The functions of **لَا**, interrogative **مَا**, and relative **مَا** are not conflated.
- Verb tense, imperatives, prohibitions, question particles, translations, case notes, and Quran references are accurately distinguished.
- The spoken-phrases lesson is coherent, register-reviewed, and does not present language practice as a source of religious rulings.
- Chapter 31 extends question-form comprehension without duplicating Chapter 22's question/repair phrases or Chapter 30's dialogue framework.
- Review and test content assess only the stated outcomes and comply with the canonical schema and existing runtime behavior.
- Fixtures validate successfully before any content synchronization or publication.
