# Chapter 27 — Common Prepositions in Context

**Status:** Proposed; not implemented or approved

**Scope:** Chapter 27 map, five current lesson fixtures, one new cumulative review, and one distinct chapter test. Stable lesson IDs are retained; the current review ID is reassigned as the fifth teaching lesson.

## 1. Decision and learning outcome

Retain Chapter 27 as a preposition chapter, but replace the claim that it teaches “all major prepositions” with a bounded goal: learners apply **eight common prepositions** in context. The chapter should build on prepositions already encountered in Chapters 16 and 21, deepen meanings and written forms, and apply the attached pronouns taught in Chapters 19–20. It should not present every preposition as new or imply that each has one English equivalent.

By the end, learners should be able to:

1. Choose between familiar location/direction prepositions by reading a complete sentence, not translating an isolated word.
2. Recognize the written forms of the attached particles **بِـ، لِـ، كَـ** before definite nouns and distinguish them from separate prepositions such as **فِي، إِلَى، مِنْ، عَلَى، عَنْ**.
3. Interpret common meanings of **بِـ، لِـ، عَلَى، مِنْ، عَنْ، كَـ** from context without treating a short gloss as exhaustive.
4. Recognize prepositions attached to familiar pronoun suffixes, such as **لَهُ، بِهِ، إِلَيْهِ، مِنْهُ، عَلَيْهِ، عَنْهُ**, and distinguish “to/for him” or “from him” from possession on a noun.
5. Read the target structures in verified Quran excerpts and identify the governed noun or pronoun.
6. Recognize that the noun after a preposition is genitive; identify case endings only where they are visible and part of the taught objective.

## 2. Curriculum fit and deliberate retrieval

| Prior learning | Chapter 27's new work | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Chapter 16 already uses **فِي، عَلَى، إِلَى، بِـ** in a school scene. | Retrieve them in contrasting complete sentences and add contextual meaning and written-form recognition. | Do not spend a full lesson teaching their basic translations again. |
| Chapter 21 already uses **مِنْ** and **إِلَى** for movement and routes. | Reuse them in new contexts, especially phrases that do not describe movement. | Do not repeat the Chapter 21 journey lesson. |
| Chapters 19–20 teach attached pronouns on nouns and actions. | Apply those known suffixes after prepositions, where the pronoun is governed by the preposition: **لَهُ، مِنْهُ**. | Briefly retrieve suffix meaning; do not reteach the pronoun paradigms. |
| Earlier lessons introduce idafa and Quranic reading. | Distinguish a preposition-governed genitive from the second noun in idafa when a useful example contains both. | Do not label every genitive noun **مضاف إليه**. |

The phrase in the current Lesson 3 hook, **رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ**, is idafa. Al-Fatihah 1:2 contains a separate lām preposition in **لِلَّهِ**; the two structures must not be conflated. This is verified by the [Quranic Arabic Corpus word-by-word analysis](https://corpus.quran.com/wordbyword.jsp?chapter=1&verse=2).

Five teaching lessons are justified here: the chapter has two different written patterns (separate words and attached particles), multiple contextual meanings, and a new application of already learned pronouns after prepositions. The fifth lesson gives learners guided reading before the review and assessment. Avoid adding a full inventory of rare prepositions or advanced semantic categories; those would need their own later scope.

## 5. Assessment blueprint

Start with 12 questions, using the product's configured pass threshold. Increase the number only if review shows that one outcome is under-sampled.

| Outcome | Questions |
|---|---:|
| Choose a location, source or destination phrase in context | 2 |
| Recognize separate versus attached prepositions | 2 |
| Infer a preposition's contextual meaning | 2 |
| Read a preposition attached to a pronoun and track its referent | 3 |
| Interpret a verified Quran excerpt without verse-trivia questions | 3 |
| **Total** | **12** |

Every question must have one defensible answer in both English and Urdu. Do not use two equivalent translations as competing options. Sentence-building questions must show correct Arabic attachment and supply every required tile. A Quran passage must actually contain the target form; a passage used only for retrieval must be labelled as such.
