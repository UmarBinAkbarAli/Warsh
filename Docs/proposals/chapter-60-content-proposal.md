# Chapter 60 — Travel, Schedules, and Hajj Conversation: Content Proposal

**Status:** Proposal only; not approved or implemented  
**Scope:** Chapter 60 map and learner-facing lessons after owner approval  
**Current sources:** `warsh-backend/prisma/curriculum-books7-8.cjs` and `warsh-backend/prisma/fixtures/chapter-60-lesson-01.json` through `chapter-60-lesson-06.json`

## Recommendation

Rebuild Chapter 60 as Book 7's first practical application unit: help a learner ask about travel, time, destinations, and Hajj-related places in clear Modern Standard Arabic. The current theme is a good transition after the Book 6 capstone, but the lesson map and fixtures have drifted apart, several vocabulary and grammar notes are wrong, and religious travel instructions are mixed into vocabulary cards without appropriate qualification.

Keep religious content as a motivating context for Arabic, not as a substitute for a Hajj guide. Separate stable language instruction from fiqh and current travel requirements. Have any ritual statement reviewed by a qualified religious content reviewer; remove prescriptive detail that is not needed to teach a word or conversation. Do not assume Chapter 59's unapproved proposal or its corrections are implemented; Chapter 60 should use Arabic that learners have actually met and avoid relying on an unapproved parse.

### Evidence notes for the Qur'anic anchors

- **Al-Baqarah 2:197** explicitly uses the plural **أَشْهُرٌ**; use it as context for dates, not to imply the phrase names only Dhul-Hijjah. [QAC word-by-word syntax](https://corpus.quran.com/treebank.jsp?chapter=2&verse=197).
- **Al-Hajj 22:27** is a call to proclaim Hajj, followed by language about people coming; the Corpus parses **رِجَالًا** as حال and **يَأْتُوكَ** as the response to an implied condition. [QAC iʿrāb](https://corpus.quran.com/grammar.jsp?chapter=22&verse=27).
- **Al-Baqarah 2:189** describes the new moons as time markers for people and Hajj; **2:198** refers to departing from ʿArafāt and remembering Allah at al-Mashʿar al-Ḥarām; **2:200** speaks of remembering Allah after completing the rites. These references should support only the text actually quoted and taught. Check each complete Arabic excerpt against a Quran source before editing it.
- Current official descriptions confirm Dhul-Hulayfah is the Madinah miqat and describe the Hajj sequence through ʿArafāt, Muzdalifah, and Mina. Ritual schedules and rulings vary in detail and can change operationally; use an official guide and an appropriate scholar, not a language fixture, for those claims. [Dhul-Hulayfah reference](https://services.prh.gov.sa/en/amakin_mokadasa.php?mode=makkah), [Ministry Hajj guides](https://haj.gov.sa/en/Awareness-Center/Awareness-Guides), [Saudi Press Agency: pilgrims proceed from ʿArafāt to Muzdalifah](https://www.spa.gov.sa/en/N2599591).

## Continuity with the wider course

- Chapter 59 is proposed as a Book 6 capstone, but its proposal remains unapproved. Chapter 60 can naturally shift from grammar analysis to everyday use; it should not claim that Book 6 is completed/mastered until the corrected sequence and its assessment are approved and implemented.
- Chapter 60 should make Book 7's promised “applied spiral” concrete through supported language tasks, not through an abrupt jump into advanced **جواب الطلب** parsing. Keep grammar explanations aligned to the approved course sequence.
- Chapter 61 is already titled **Trade, Weights, and Instrument Nouns**. Commerce vocabulary belongs there; Chapter 60 should not spend its main conversation on trade-like reminders, confirmations, or instrument derivations.
- Reuse the course's conversation-lab approach intentionally: practice has a real goal (plan a departure, locate a place, clarify a time), several turns, comprehensible answer choices, and a chance to recover from misunderstanding. A single prompted answer is not yet a “conversation.”

## Proposed lesson sequence

Six regular lessons plus a formative review and a distinct final test are recommended. Keep the chapter's existing practical scope, but redistribute and correct it:

1. **Travel essentials and getting around** (`STANDARD`, revise). Teach a compact set of transport and destination words. Build phrases such as **أَيْنَ الْمَطَارُ؟**, **أُرِيدُ تَذْكِرَةً إِلَى مَكَّةَ**, and **مَتَى تَصِلُ؟** only after native/qualified Arabic review. Remove the “shape of ط” explanation and correct all English/Urdu glosses.
2. **Dates, appointments, and travel time** (`STANDARD`, rebuild). Practice asking when a journey leaves/arrives and how long it takes; teach days, months, hours, and a small number of natural time phrases. Reuse **مَوْعِدٌ، سَاعَةٌ، يَوْمٌ، شَهْرٌ** accurately. Use the 2:189 excerpt to notice **مَوَاقِيت** in Quranic context without asserting unsupported fiqh.
3. **Hajj and ʿUmrah words** (`STANDARD`, rebuild). Teach the difference as vocabulary and context, alongside **إِحْرَامٌ** and **مِيقَاتٌ**. Correct the plural/location error; distinguish the men's garments from the broader state of iḥrām. Avoid current permit claims and unreviewed ritual instructions.
4. **A travel conversation: plan and confirm a trip** (`STANDARD`, rebuild). Use a multi-turn role-play between traveler and service provider/companion: destination, day/time, transport, confirmation, and one clarification/repair. Include natural Modern Standard Arabic, audio, and a meaningful response choice rather than four translation-only distractors.
5. **Finding and naming the sacred places** (`STANDARD`, rebuild). Recycle **مَكَّة، مِنًى، عَرَفَات، مُزْدَلِفَة** in map/location language, with one short, carefully bounded Quranic context (for example 2:198). Keep sequence/rule statements out unless they have religious review. Do not define **جَمْرَة** as “pillar.”
6. **Integrated Hajj/travel help dialogue** (`STANDARD`, new/rebuild). Let the learner ask where to go, ask for repetition, confirm a time/place, and respond politely. Include a simple task such as matching a spoken announcement to a destination and then choosing a suitable follow-up. Use 22:27 as a reading/listening context only, not a new lesson on jussive governance.
7. **Travel and Hajj retrieval review** (`REVIEW`, new). Formative, explanation-rich review of the words, time expressions, place names, and exchanges actually taught. Recycle across both themes and correct misconceptions; it does not control chapter completion.
8. **Chapter 60 final test** (`REVIEW`, new). A separate canonical `CHAPTER_TEST`, mapped to approved outcomes and used by backend chapter-completion logic. Test recognition and contextual use, not religious-law details or unintroduced grammar. Include listening/meaning, time/destination interpretation, and selecting/building a valid dialogue response. Set item count and pass threshold from the approved blueprint and existing product conventions.

If owner-approved scope or learner level requires fewer items, combine lessons only when their outcomes and practice remain clear. Do not preserve six just for symmetry, and do not expand the Hajj lesson into a ritual manual merely to fill lesson slots.

## Assessment and editorial requirements

Before building the final test, make a blueprint linking each question to (1) the lesson and objective where it was taught, (2) the receptive or productive skill tested, (3) its correct response and acceptable variants, and (4) why each distractor is wrong. Use audio only if the prompt and recording match the intended dialect/register and the lesson is actually assessing listening. Provide helpful feedback in English and Urdu. Do not score unsupervised voice recording or assess fiqh correctness in an Arabic-language test.

Review every Arabic phrase for spelling, diacritics, naturalness, register, and target-language correctness; every Quran excerpt for exact text/reference/translation/audio; all English and Urdu independently; every prompt, distractor and answer key; and all word-family, plural, and gender claims. Have a qualified Arabic reviewer check the map parse and dialogue. Have a qualified religious reviewer check references to miqats, ihram garments, ritual sequence, Jamaraat, shaving/trimming, and farewell tawaf. Operational details should be sourced to current official guidance and dated where needed.

## Acceptance criteria

- The chapter map and all fixtures have the same title, learning outcomes, lesson count, and order.
- There are six purposeful learning lessons, one formative `REVIEW`, and a distinct final `REVIEW` containing `assessment.type: "CHAPTER_TEST"` and `chapter_order: 60`.
- The learner can complete at least two multi-turn role-plays with useful comprehension, response, and repair practice; “conversation” is not represented by one isolated selection.
- **ذُو الْحُجَّةِ** is not labeled a miqat; the Madinah miqat is correctly named **ذُو الْحُلَيْفَة** if retained, and the plural is checked.
- **مَهْدِيّ / مُهَيْد**, **إِرْحَال**, the erroneous **أَجَلُ الْحَجّ** collocation, the false **غَيْر** part of speech, folk etymologies, and stray off-topic content are removed or replaced with verified, useful language.
- The Quranic anchors are aligned to their actual quoted wording and lesson goals; grammar not yet taught is not claimed as mastered.
- Hajj references are accurate, appropriately qualified, scholarly reviewed, and clearly distinguished from the language-learning objective.
- English, Urdu, Arabic, transliteration, audio, exercise feedback, response keys, and content metadata are checked together.

After approval and implementation, validate with the lesson schema, Quran-text audit, Urdu audit, and assessment/backend completion tests. Run `npm run content:check` before any fixture sync. This proposal authorizes no fixture edits, database writes, content synchronization, or publication.
