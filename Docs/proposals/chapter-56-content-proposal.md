# Chapter 56 — Special Noun Forms and Their Case Signs

**Status:** Proposal only — not approved or implemented  
**Evidence reviewed:** Active product/technical specifications; Chapter 56's map entry and all nine lesson fixtures; Chapter 55's proposal for prerequisite alignment; and the Chapter 57 map and opening fixture to check for duplicated scope. Quran text and selected grammar claims were checked against the Quranic Arabic Corpus and university/academic grammar references linked below. No fixture, database, or curriculum-map content has been changed.

## Recommendation

Rebuild Chapter 56 as a focused continuation of Chapter 55: learners already know the basic nominal cases, and now learn why several noun patterns show those cases differently. The chapter map names four targets—**al-maqṣūr**, **al-manqūṣ**, the five nouns, and the dual—but the current fixtures repeatedly teach false rules, promise that unrelated material becomes fully parseable, and append an inaccurate lesson on the five verbs, which Chapter 57 owns.

Keep this chapter to those four noun patterns. Use **seven focused instructional lessons**, followed by a formative mixed review and a separate final `CHAPTER_TEST`. The seven lessons should give each pattern an accurate first explanation, enough controlled practice, and a cumulative Quranic application. Remove the current Chapter 56 lesson on **al-afʿāl al-khamsa**; teach that topic only in Chapter 57, where it belongs, and correct it there rather than carrying its present errors forward. Do not add a lesson just to preserve the current nine-lesson count.

## Continuity and scope

- **Chapter 55** is intended to establish iʿrāb, the core noun cases, and common roles. Its proposal is not yet an approved curriculum change, so verify actual learner prerequisites before implementation. If learners have not been taught case versus syntactic role, give a brief retrieval bridge here rather than assuming the proposal was adopted.
- **Chapter 56** owns four special noun topics: estimated endings on maqṣūr nouns, the case behavior of manqūṣ nouns, the conditional letter-based case signs of the five nouns, and the dual noun endings.
- **Chapter 57** explicitly owns weak verbs and the five imperfect-verb forms. Chapter 56 must not teach these as a fifth noun type or make verb mood a side quest in a noun chapter.
- **Chapter 58** is the higher-syntax unit. Chapter 56's cumulative parse should use only structures already taught; it must not imply that a whole surah is now completely parseable.
- The map's Al-Humazah 104:1 hook is not a useful illustration of the four special noun patterns. It may remain a separate Tadabbur unlock/read, but it cannot be presented as proof that these categories are found there or that Chapter 56 makes the surah “fully accessible.” A better cumulative example for the dual and one of the five nouns is **تَبَّتْ يَدَا أَبِي لَهَبٍ** (Al-Masad 111:1): the Corpus identifies **يَدَا** as a nominative feminine dual noun and **أَبِي** as a genitive noun. [QAC: يَدَا](https://corpus.quran.com/wordmorphology.jsp?location=%28111%3A1%3A2%29), [QAC: أَبِي](https://corpus.quran.com/wordmorphology.jsp?location=%28111%3A1%3A3%29).

### Critical and high-priority issues

| Priority | Issue | Proposed fix |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Lessons 1 and 6 say a maqṣūr noun's case vowels appear on the **ي before the alif**. For a regular maqṣūr noun, the short case endings are **estimated on the final alif** because they cannot be pronounced there; the spelling **ى** is alif maqṣūra, not a yāʾ that takes the short vowels. The claim that the final alif itself “absorbs” a visible change is also misleading. | Teach a regular common noun such as **الْفَتَى** in three controlled sentence roles: **جَاءَ الْفَتَى، رَأَيْتُ الْفَتَى، مَرَرْتُ بِالْفَتَى**. The written ending stays the same; identify the estimated ḍamma/fatḥa/kasra on the final alif from syntax. Avoid using **مُوسَى** as the sole proof: a proper name adds declension/indeclinability complications beyond the intended rule. Distinguish the written alif maqṣūra **ى** from the letter yāʾ **ي**. |
| Critical | Lesson 1's **نَصَرَ مُوسَى** parse says **مُوسَى** is genitive after a verb and “مفعول له or similar.” It is the direct object, in the accusative syntactic position; the example does not teach genitive. Lesson 1 also claims **عِيسَى** has a regular triliteral root **ع-ي-س-ي** and labels its morphology inconsistently. | Replace malformed examples with transparent, reviewed sentences and label case, role, and visible/estimated sign separately. If a Quranic proper name is retained, have an Arabic morphology reviewer verify its lexical classification and do not use a guessed root. |
| Critical | Lessons 2 and 6 define **اسم منقوص** by saying yāʾ is a “fourth root letter” and that it drops in the nominative, but then show it still present in every written form. They miss the key distinction between a definite/construct form (e.g. **الْقَاضِي**) and an indefinite form (e.g. **قَاضٍ** in nominative/genitive, **قَاضِيًا** in accusative). With a definite manqūṣ noun, ḍamma and kasra are estimated on the yāʾ; fatḥa is apparent. In the indefinite nominative/genitive, the yāʾ is omitted with compensatory kasratayn. QAC's **هَادٍ** in 13:7 demonstrates the indefinite nominative pattern. [QAC 13:7](https://corpus.quran.com/grammar.jsp?chapter=13&verse=7). | Define it accurately as a declinable noun ending in a yāʾ preceded by kasra, then teach definite and indefinite forms in a side-by-side table across rafʿ, naṣb, and jarr. Use **الْقَاضِي / قَاضٍ / قَاضِيًا** with fully vocalized, reviewer-checked examples. Say “the yāʾ is omitted in the indefinite nominative/genitive form,” not that it disappears from every nominative manqūṣ spelling. |
| Critical | Lessons 3 and 7 say the five nouns drop **و/ي** in iḍāfa and restore them as long vowels with pronouns; they call **ذُو** fixed/mabnī on ḍamma. The normal teaching rule is that, when its conditions are met, the noun is declinable by letters: **أَبُو / أَبَا / أَبِي** (and corresponding forms such as **ذُو / ذَا / ذِي**). The letter is a case marker, not a vowel restored after deletion. Qabas, Birzeit University's Arabic lexicon, lists the five nouns and their wāw/alif/yāʾ case signs. [Qabas: الأسماء الخمسة](https://sina.birzeit.edu/qabas/lemma/2021862800), [Qabas: ذو](https://sina.birzeit.edu/qabas/lemma/202004487). | Replace the “dropped then restored” story with a case table and sentence roles, e.g. **جَاءَ أَبُوكَ / رَأَيْتُ أَبَاكَ / مَرَرْتُ بِأَبِيكَ**. Explain the conditions at an appropriate level: singular, in iḍāfa, not annexed to yāʾ al-mutakallim, and not diminutive; explain the special spelling **فُو** separately. Treat **ذُو** as declinable, meaning “possessor of,” and always show its complement. Have a qualified reviewer confirm the exact scope/conditions before publication. |

The examples do not teach the stated target. | Keep ordinary iḍāfa with an attached pronoun only as a contrast, and explicitly label **رَبّ** as a regular noun. Use the five-noun forms themselves for the lesson objective, including how the attached pronoun is the mudāf ilayh while the long letter marks case on the noun. Verify the source and full parse of any Quran excerpt. |
| Critical | Lessons 4 and 5 say the dual applies to nouns **and verbs**, then describe a past verb's dual ending as **ـَانِ**. The noun/adjective dual has **ـانِ** in rafʿ and **ـيْنِ** in naṣb/jarr; a past verb uses the dual subject suffix **ـا** (e.g. **ذَهَبَا**), which is not nominal dual case inflection. None is one of the four target special noun patterns. ## Proposed lesson plan

1. **A quick case-sign reset** (`STANDARD`, revised/short): Retrieve only the Chapter 55 distinction needed here: role determines case; ordinary nouns often show short-vowel signs, while some special patterns use estimated marks or letters. Do not reteach the whole chapter.
2. **Al-maqṣūr: endings estimated on alif** (`STANDARD`, revised): Define final alif maqṣūra/alif in the relevant noun class. Use **الْفَتَى** in subject, object, and after-preposition roles to show the same spelling with estimated ḍamma, fatḥa, and kasra on the final alif. Distinguish **ى** from **ي**; do not claim a visible vowel sits on the yāʾ before alif.
3. **Al-manqūṣ: when yāʾ remains and when it is omitted** (`STANDARD`, revised): Define the pattern precisely and contrast definite **الْقَاضِي** with indefinite **قَاضٍ / قَاضِيًا** across case. Teach estimated ḍamma/kasra versus apparent fatḥa and the indefinite yāʾ omission with kasratayn. Use Quran 13:7's **هَادٍ** only as a carefully supported application, not a replacement for the pattern table.
4. **The five nouns: the case letters** (`STANDARD`, rebuilt): Establish **أب، أخ، حم، فو، ذو** and the core paradigm: wāw for rafʿ, alif for naṣb, yāʾ for jarr, with clean constructed examples such as **أبوك / أباك / أبيك**.
5. **The five nouns in iḍāfa and context** (`STANDARD`, revised): Practise the declension conditions; distinguish the case-marking letter from the attached possessive pronoun; include **ذو** with its complement and explain **فو** carefully. Add one verified Quranic application, such as **أَبِي** in 111:1, only after reviewer confirmation of its role and the already-taught iḍāfa prerequisite.
6. **The dual noun: ـانِ and ـيْنِ** (`STANDARD`, revised): Teach singular-to-dual formation, rafʿ versus naṣb/jarr, adjective agreement, and deletion of nūn in iḍāfa. State explicitly that this is noun/adjective inflection, not the morphology of a dual verb.
7. **Read the special forms in context** (`STANDARD`, rebuilt): Guided cumulative application to **تَبَّتْ يَدَا أَبِي لَهَبٍ** (111:1): identify **يَدَا** as dual nominative and **أَبِي** as a genitive form of one of the five nouns, while marking other structures only to the extent already taught. Include a second short transfer item for maqṣūr/manqūṣ using controlled examples. Do not claim a complete surah parse.
8. **Chapter 56 retrieval review** (`REVIEW`, new/rebuilt): Mixed, explanatory retrieval across the four noun patterns, case roles, conditions, definite/indefinite distinctions, and dual iḍāfa. Keep this formative and distinct from the final test.
9. **Chapter 56 checkpoint** (`REVIEW`, new): A separate, schema-valid `CHAPTER_TEST` assessment mapped to the four noun outcomes. Follow the canonical assessment/completion behavior; do not invent test length or pass threshold in this proposal.

## Acceptance criteria

- The chapter map and lessons agree on four noun-pattern outcomes; Chapter 56 contains no teaching lesson on **الأفعال الخمسة**.
- Maqṣūr examples place all three estimated case marks on the final alif in the explanation, not on a preceding yāʾ; the material distinguishes **ى** and **ي**.
- Manqūṣ teaching accurately distinguishes definite forms and indefinite **قاضٍ / قاضيًا** behavior, including which signs are estimated and when yāʾ is omitted.
- The five nouns are declinable—not mabnī—and the case letters, conditions, iḍāfa, and attached-pronoun roles are taught without “restore the dropped vowel” explanations.
- Dual noun/adjective endings are not confused with dual verb suffixes; the nūn deletion rule in iḍāfa is clear and correctly exemplified.
- Al-Humazah 104:1 is not described as containing the special noun patterns or as newly “fully parseable”; **وَيْلٌ** is not called mabnī, and **هُمَزَةٍ / لُمَزَةٍ** are not called accusative.
- The Al-Masad 111:1 application labels **يَدَا** and **أَبِي** accurately and makes only claims the learner has been prepared to understand.
- Chapter completion copy no longer precedes later teaching; there is a formative review and a separate, canonical `CHAPTER_TEST` assessment.
- English/Urdu parity, Arabic text, source metadata, grammar labels, answer keys, distractors, explanations, and audio/highlights have been reviewed together.
